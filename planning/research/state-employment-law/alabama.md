# Alabama Employment Law

**State:** Alabama | **Researched:** 2026-09-03 | **Status:** REVIEWED

## Summary for Human Capital Management Suite

- Alabama is a strict at-will employment state with very limited statutory exceptions; termination without cause is permitted.
- No state minimum wage, overtime, or mandated pay frequency laws; federal FLSA minimum of $7.25/hour applies.
- Clarke-Figures Equal Pay Act (Ala. Code § 25-1-30) prohibits wage discrimination based on sex or race for substantially similar work; requires 3-year wage record retention.
- No state mandate for paid sick leave, family leave, or personal leave in private sector; federal FMLA applies to employers with 50+ employees within 75-mile radius.
- Workers' compensation retaliation protection (Ala. Code § 25-5-11.1) prohibits termination "solely because" of workers' compensation claim using a strict, narrow standard.
- Non-compete and non-solicit agreements enforceable under Ala. Code § 8-1-190 et seq. (effective 2016): 2-year presumption for non-competes, 18-month presumption for non-solicits.
- Federal WARN Act applies to employers with 100+ employees in Alabama; no state mini-WARN.
- Right-to-work state (Ala. Code § 25-7-2); union membership and dues cannot be required as condition of employment.
- E-Verify mandatory for all employers under Beason-Hammon Act (Ala. Code § 31-13-15), effective April 1, 2012; no employer-size carve-outs.
- As of July 1, 2025, overtime income fully subject to Alabama state income tax; prior exemption (Act 2024-382) expired June 30, 2025.

## Employment Relationship

**At-Will Employment Status:**

Alabama law establishes at-will employment as the default relationship. Employers may terminate employees with or without cause, with limited statutory exceptions. Alabama courts have explicitly rejected a generalized public policy exception to at-will employment; only legislatively enacted exceptions apply.

**Statutory Exceptions to At-Will:**

- **Workers' Compensation Retaliation Protection (Ala. Code § 25-5-11.1):** Employers are prohibited from terminating an employee "solely because" the employee has instituted or maintained any action against the employer to recover workers' compensation benefits or solely because the employee has filed a written notice of violation of a safety rule. Alabama courts apply a strict "solely because" standard; mixed-motive discharges typically fail this exception. Violation allows employee to sue for compensatory and punitive damages and is triable to a jury.
- **Jury Duty Protection (Ala. Code § 12-16-8):** Employers must excuse full-time employees summoned for jury duty without requiring vacation or sick leave use. Employees receive usual compensation during jury service. Small employer exception: Courts may postpone jury duty for employees of firms with ≤5 full-time employees if another employee also summoned.
- **Voting Leave (Ala. Code § 17-1-5):** Employees may take up to one hour of unpaid time off to vote in any municipal, county, state, or federal election or primary, provided reasonable notice is given and the employee's work schedule does not already accommodate voting (i.e., hours commence ≥2 hours after polls open or end ≥1 hour before polls close). Employer may specify hours during which voting time may be taken.
- **Military Service (Ala. Code § 31-12):** Aligned with federal USERRA: up to 5 years unpaid military leave with reemployment in same or comparable job. Alabama National Guard State Duty Exception: same USERRA protections apply for state active duty ≥30 consecutive days or federally funded duty.
- **Federal Protections:** Title VII (race, color, religion, sex, national origin), ADA (disability), ADEA (age 40+; Alabama Age Discrimination in Employment Act § 25-1-20 applies to employers with 20+ employees), GINA (genetic information), USERRA (military service).

**Required Written Notices:**

No state law requires written notice of hire, termination, or pay/role change terms at hire. Federal FLSA applies: employers must inform employees of rates and pay periods.

**Wage Theft Prevention:**

No state wage theft prevention notice requirement. Federal FLSA notice applies.

## Wages

**Minimum Wage:**

No state minimum wage statute; federal FLSA minimum of $7.25/hour applies.

**Overtime:**

No state overtime statute; federal FLSA 1.5x overtime for hours exceeding 40/week applies. **Prior Tax Exemption Expired:** Overtime income was temporarily exempt from Alabama state income tax under Act 2024-382 (effective October 1, 2024, through June 30, 2025, with expanded salaried-worker coverage). The exemption expired July 1, 2025. As of 2026, overtime income is fully subject to Alabama state income tax withholding. Employers must include overtime pay in state income tax withholding calculations.

**Pay Frequency:**

No state-mandated pay frequency law. Employers may pay weekly, biweekly, semimonthly, or monthly. Federal FLSA default applies: final paycheck paid on next regularly scheduled payday.

**Final Paycheck on Termination:**

No state final paycheck statute. Employees paid on next regularly scheduled payday regardless of separation. **Commission Exception:** Commissions due at termination must be paid within 30 days of termination (verify: exact statute citation).

**Permitted Deductions:**

No state-specific statute governing permitted deductions beyond federal FLSA rules. Employers must obtain employee consent for deductions in writing; deductions cannot reduce pay below minimum wage.

**Pay Statement Content:**

No state pay statement content requirements. Federal FLSA applies: employees must be able to verify wages earned and hours worked.

## Pay Transparency and Equity

**Clarke-Figures Equal Pay Act (Ala. Code § 25-1-30, effective Sept 1, 2019):**

Prohibits wage rate discrimination based on sex or race for "substantially similar work" when viewed as a composite of skill, effort, responsibility, and similar working conditions. Applies to public and private employers. Requires employers to retain records of wages, wage rates, job classifications, and employment terms and conditions for 3 years. No specified enforcement mechanism exists for private employees; civil remedy may be available via common law.

**Salary History Bans:**

No state law restricts use of salary history in hiring decisions. Employers may ask applicants about prior salary; no ban exists.

**Wage Transparency in Postings:**

No state requirement to post or disclose salary ranges in job postings or upon request. Federal only.

**Pay Data Reporting:**

No state pay equity audit or pay data reporting requirement. Federal EEO-1 applies (employers with 100+ employees).

## Leave and Time

**Paid Sick Leave (Private Sector):**

No state mandate for private sector paid sick leave. Employers free to establish own policies. Exception: State employees receive 4 hours sick leave per biweekly service period (public sector only).

**Family Leave:**

Federal FMLA applies (employees at employers with 50+ employees within 75-mile radius, 12+ months employment, 1,250+ hours worked in prior year). 12 weeks unpaid, job-protected. No state FMLA.

**Paid Parental Leave (Public Sector):**

Alabama Public Employee Paid Parental Leave Act (effective July 1, 2025): Up to 8 weeks paid parental leave for mothers; up to 2 weeks paid parental leave for fathers or adoption. Private sector: no state requirement.

**Personal Leave:**

No state mandate for personal leave days. Federal jury duty exemption applies (Ala. Code § 12-16-8).

**Military Service Leave:**

Federal USERRA applies: up to 5 years unpaid military leave, reemployment in same or comparable job. Alabama National Guard State Duty Exception: Same USERRA protections apply for state active duty ≥30 consecutive days or federally funded duty.

**Interaction with Pay or Role Changes:**

Leave usage is controlled by employer policy and federal law. Promotion or pay change does not reset leave accrual unless policy explicitly provides. No state law addresses interaction.

## Records and Access

**Payroll Record Retention:**

Minimum 3-year retention of wages, pay periods, hours worked, job titles, classifications, pay rates, and employment terms and conditions (required by equal pay law, Ala. Code § 25-1-30).

**Personnel File Access:**

No state law grants employees right to inspect personnel files. Federal privacy law applies (verify: Alabama code for any state personnel privacy statute).

**Unemployment Records:**

Minimum 5-year retention.

**Workers' Compensation Records:**

Minimum 12-year retention for all injury records (fatal and non-fatal) where payment claimed or made.

**Record Format:**

No state requirement for electronic format; paper or electronic acceptable.

## Privacy and Data

**State Privacy Law:**

Alabama has no comprehensive consumer or employee privacy statute; the Alabama Data Breach Notification Act of 2018 (Ala. Code § 8-38-1 et seq.) covers breach notification only and **excludes employee data** from its core requirements. Limited breach notification obligation for employee data applies only to biometric data with name; 45-day notification required.

**Biometric Data:**

No state biometric privacy law restricting employer collection of biometric data. Employers may collect fingerprints, facial recognition, and other biometric data without consent or notice. Federal only.

**Workplace Monitoring:**

No state electronic monitoring or surveillance law limiting employer collection of communications or location data. Federal baseline applies (verify: Alabama code for any camera/audio recording restrictions).

**Breach Notification (Ala. Code § 8-38-5):**

Alabama Data Breach Notification Act of 2018 (effective June 1, 2018) requires notification to affected residents within 45 days of determining a breach occurred. Notification requirement applies only if breach is "reasonably likely to cause substantial harm to residents." Notification to Alabama Attorney General required if more than 1,000 residents affected. For employee data, biometric data breach with name requires notification. Written determination that breach poses no substantial harm must be documented and retained for 5 years.

**Data Residency or Processing Constraints:**

No state requirement. Federal compliance applies.

## Hiring and Background

**Ban-the-Box:**

No statewide ban-the-box law for private employers. Local exception: City of Birmingham (2016 executive order) applies to city government jobs only, not private employers.

**Background Checks:**

Governed by federal FCRA only. No state mini-FCRA or additional restrictions.

**Drug Testing:**

Permitted. Employers may establish drug testing and drug-free workplace policies. Employers may prohibit cannabis use and enforce through testing. **Drug-Free Workplace Program (Ala. Code § 25-5-330 et seq.):** Employers who certify a drug-free workplace program with the Alabama Department of Labor receive 5% workers' compensation insurance premium discount, applied pro rata for up to 4 years. Program must include substance abuse testing (pre-offer, reasonable suspicion, post-accident, fitness-for-duty, and follow-up testing provisions), Employee Assistance Program, employee education, and supervisor training.

**E-Verify:**

**Mandatory for All Employers (Beason-Hammon Alabama Taxpayer and Citizen Protection Act, Ala. Code § 31-13-15, effective April 1, 2012):** All Alabama employers—public and private, regardless of size—must verify employment eligibility using E-Verify (verify.alabama.gov) for every new hire. No employer-size exceptions. Three business days from hire date allowed to open E-Verify case. Employer violation subject to contract debarment and license suspension. Employers using E-Verify have immunity from liability for knowingly employing unauthorized aliens.

**Credit Checks:**

No state restrictions on employer credit report use.

## Separation

**Severance:**

No state severance requirement. Severance may be required by contract or collective bargaining agreement. Note: May be needed for enforceability of non-compete with existing employees in some Alabama circuits.

**Non-Compete Agreements (Ala. Code § 8-1-190 et seq., effective Jan. 1, 2016):**

General rule: contracts restraining lawful profession, trade, or business are void. Exceptions permit non-competes in specific circumstances if they meet statutory requirements. Must be in writing and signed by employer and employee. For existing employees (not new hires), independent consideration beyond continued employment is typically required in most Alabama circuits.

**Presumptions of Reasonableness:**

- Employee non-competes ≤2 years presumed reasonable
- Business sale non-competes ≤1 year presumed reasonable
- Agreement must be reasonably related to protectable business interest, reasonable in geographic scope, and not impose undue hardship on employee

**Non-Solicit Agreements (Ala. Code § 8-1-191):**

Agreements restricting solicitation of current customers presumed reasonable if duration ≤18 months or as long as post-employment consideration is paid, whichever is longer. Must be in writing and signed.

**Mini-WARN Act:**

No state mini-WARN. Federal WARN Act applies: employers with 100+ employees must provide 60-day advance notice of mass layoffs (50+ workers) or plant closings to affected employees, Alabama Department of Labor, and chief elected local official. Notice must include expected separation date and schedule. Back pay and benefits owed for missed notice period.

## Classification and Multi-State

**Independent Contractor Test:**

Alabama Act 2021-226 (effective 2021) adopted the **IRS Common Law "Right of Control" Test** (20-factor analysis) for wage/hour, unemployment, and income tax purposes. Does not apply to workers' compensation classification, which uses traditional right-of-control standard (Ala. Code § 25-5-2).

**Three Prongs of Common Law Test:**

1. Behavioral control: Does company control how worker performs tasks?
2. Financial control: Worker's investment, profit/loss potential, multiple clients?
3. Relationship factors: How do parties view the relationship?

**Safe Harbor:**

Section 530 protection available if employer had reasonable basis for IC classification, issued 1099s, and history of consistent IC treatment.

**Remote Work:**

No state-specific telecommuting law. Tax benefit: Nonresident employees working <30 days in Alabama exempt from state income tax withholding (2026 rule). ADA may require remote work as reasonable accommodation. No telecommuting tax credit.

**Multi-State Worker Implications:**

Businesses in multiple states must comply with conflicting classification tests (Alabama common law via Act 2021-226 vs. ABC test in other states). Platform must flag when same worker crosses state lines.

## Implications for P1A/P1B

**Must Model as Typed Obligation or Notice:**

- **Equal Pay Audit Trail:** Capture job classifications, skills, effort, responsibility, and working conditions for each promotion or pay change; enable proof of "substantially similar work" evaluation and document the sex and race protected bases the Act covers (Ala. Code § 25-1-30).
- **Wage Record Retention:** Automatically retain all wage, pay rate, job classification, and term/condition records for 3-year minimum; flag records older than 3 years for archive review.
- **Workers' Compensation Termination Restriction:** Flag if termination reason is workers' compensation claim or safety report; block or require manager acknowledgment if "solely because" reason detected (Ala. Code § 25-5-11.1); a § 25-5-11.1 retaliatory-discharge claim is bounded by Alabama's 2-year tort limitations period (Ala. Code § 6-2-38).
- **Non-Compete Enforceability Check:** For existing-employee non-compete enforcement, confirm independent consideration has been documented (beyond mere continued employment).
- **Classification Audit:** Document the 20-factor right-of-control analysis for any contractor reclassification; flag if historical 1099 treatment conflicts with new classification (Act 2021-226).
- **E-Verify Compliance:** All new-hire records must confirm E-Verify case opened within 3 business days of hire date; no employer-size exceptions (Ala. Code § 31-13-15).

**Must Model as Timing Constraint:**

- **Commission Payment:** 30-day payment deadline for commissions earned at termination or thereafter (verify exact statute citation).
- **Overtime Tax Withholding:** As of July 1, 2025, overtime income subject to state income tax; update payroll withholding tables for all Alabama employees.
- **Breach Notification (Biometric):** 45-day notification deadline for breaches exposing biometric data with name (Ala. Code § 8-38-5).

**Must Model as Field Restriction:**

- **At-Will Acknowledgment:** Require manager to confirm Alabama at-will employment applies (except jury duty, voting, workers' compensation retaliation).
- **Right-to-Work Compliance:** Prohibit any employer action conditioning employment on union membership or dues payment (Ala. Code § 25-7-2).
