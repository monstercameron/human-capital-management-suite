# District of Columbia Employment Law Research

**Jurisdiction:** District of Columbia | **Researched:** 2026-09-13 | **Status:** DRAFTED

## 1. Summary for Human Capital Management Suite

- **Minimum wage $17.95/hr (2026)**: $17.95/hr effective 2025-07-01, rising to
  $18.40/hr effective 2026-07-01; applies to all employers regardless of size
  (D.C. Code § 32-1003; Fair Shot Minimum Wage Amendment Act of 2016). Tipped
  base $10.00/hr (2025-07-01), $10.30/hr (2026-07-01), on a schedule to 75% of
  the standard minimum by 2034-07-01; tips plus base must equal the full
  minimum or the employer makes up the difference (§ 32-1003(f)).
- **Wage Theft Prevention notice**: Written "Notice of Hire" at hire, and an
  updated written notice within 30 days of any change to the notice's contents
  (pay rate, payday, employer information); $500 administrative penalty per
  employee not given the notice (D.C. Code § 32-1008; Wage Theft Prevention
  Amendment Act of 2014, effective 2015-02-26).
- **Pay transparency**: Good-faith minimum and maximum salary or hourly pay in
  all job listings and position descriptions, including promotion and transfer
  opportunities; healthcare-benefits existence disclosed before the first
  interview; wage-history screening banned (D.C. Code § 32-1453.01,
  § 32-1452; Wage Transparency Omnibus Amendment Act of 2023, D.C. Law 25-138,
  effective 2024-06-30).
- **Universal Paid Leave**: Employer-funded benefit financed by a 0.75% payroll
  contribution on covered wages; up to 12 weeks of paid parental, family-care,
  or medical leave administered by DOES (D.C. Code § 32-541.01 et seq.).
- **Accrued sick and safe leave**: 1 hour per 37 hours worked (100+ employees,
  cap 7 days/year), 1 per 43 (25–99, cap 5 days), 1 per 87 (24 or fewer, cap 3
  days); tipped restaurant/bar employees always accrue at the 25–99 rate
  (D.C. Code § 32-531.02).
- **Near-total non-compete ban**: Non-competes are void for employees below the
  "minimum qualifying annual compensation" ($150,000, or $250,000 for medical
  specialists, CPI-adjusted annually from 2024) for agreements entered on or
  after 2022-10-01; permitted agreements with highly compensated employees
  require statutory notice (D.C. Code § 32-581.01 et seq.; Ban on Non-Compete
  Agreements Amendment Act of 2020 as amended by the Non-Compete Clarification
  Amendment Act of 2022, D.C. Law 24-175).
- **Final pay next working day on discharge**: Discharged employees must be
  paid no later than the working day after discharge (4-day window when the
  employee is responsible for the employer's money); resignation pays the
  earlier of the next regular payday or 7 days; 10%-per-working-day liquidated
  damages capped at treble wages (D.C. Code § 32-1303).
- **Ban-the-box**: Employers with 11+ employees may not inquire into criminal
  history until after a conditional offer (Fair Criminal Record Screening
  Amendment Act of 2014, D.C. Code § 32-1341 et seq.).
- **Cannabis protections**: Employers may not refuse to hire or take adverse
  action for recreational or medical cannabis use, and may not require
  pre-employment marijuana testing, except for safety-sensitive positions
  (Cannabis Employment Protections Amendment Act of 2022, D.C. Law 24-109).
- **Breach notification**: Notice to affected residents "as expeditiously as
  possible and without unreasonable delay"; notice to the Attorney General when
  50+ D.C. residents are affected; 18 months of identity-theft protection when
  Social Security numbers are breached (D.C. Code § 28-3852, as amended by the
  Security Breach Protection Amendment Act of 2019, effective 2020).
- **Personnel file**: No private-sector statutory right to inspect a personnel
  file; no state separation-filing form identified for private employers
  (verify).

## 2. Employment Relationship: At-Will Status and Exceptions

The District of Columbia follows employment at will; either party may end the
relationship without cause. There is no statute displacing the default.

**Exceptions**:

- The D.C. Human Rights Act (D.C. Code § 2-1402.11) prohibits termination on a
  broad set of protected traits — among the widest in the country (race, color,
  religion, national origin, sex, age, marital status, personal appearance,
  sexual orientation, gender identity or expression, family responsibilities,
  matriculation, political affiliation, disability, source of income, place of
  residence or business, status as a victim of an intrafamily offense, credit
  information, and others).
- Common-law public-policy wrongful discharge exists but is construed narrowly
  and is generally unavailable where a statute supplies its own remedy.

**Required written notices at hire and on change**:

- **Notice of Hire (WTPAA)**: every employer must furnish each employee, at
  hire, a written notice containing the employer's name (and any names under
  which it does business), physical address of the main office or principal
  place of business, telephone number, the employee's rate of pay and the basis
  of that rate (hour, shift, day, week), overtime rate, allowances claimed
  (meal, lodging, tip), the regular payday, and other terms the Mayor
  prescribes; a signed, dated copy acknowledged by the employee must be
  retained as proof of compliance (D.C. Code § 32-1008(c)).
- **Notice of change**: an updated written notice within 30 days of any change
  to the information in the prior notice (§ 32-1008(d)(1)(A)). A promotion or
  base-pay change alters the stated rate and therefore triggers the updated
  notice.
- Penalty: $500 per employee for failure to provide the notice (DOES
  enforcement guidance).
- Temporary staffing firms carry an extended notice duty (§ 32-1008.01).

## 3. Wages: Minimum Wage, Overtime, Pay Frequency, Final Pay, Deductions

**Minimum wage**:

- $17.95/hr for all employees effective 2025-07-01; $18.40/hr effective
  2026-07-01, regardless of employer size (§ 32-1003(a); DOES 2026 Minimum
  Wage Increase Notice).
- Tipped base: $10.00/hr effective 2025-07-01, $10.30/hr effective 2026-07-01;
  thereafter a percentage schedule of the standard minimum (56% in 2026, 60%
  2028, 65% 2030, 70% 2032, 75% 2034); weekly-averaged tips plus base must
  equal the full minimum (§ 32-1003(f), as amended by D.C. Law 26-55).
- Annual increases track CPI for the Washington metropolitan area, adjusted to
  the nearest $0.05 (§ 32-1003(f)).

**Overtime**:

- 1.5x the regular rate for hours worked over 40 in a workweek (§ 32-1003(c));
  no daily overtime rule.

**Pay frequency**:

- Wages must be paid at least twice per month on regular paydays designated in
  advance; payment within 10 working days after the close of the pay period
  (D.C. Code § 32-1302).

**Final pay**:

- Discharge: all wages earned no later than the working day following the
  discharge (§ 32-1303(1)).
- Discharge/resignation where the employee is responsible for the employer's
  money: 4 days to determine account accuracy, then all wages due
  (§ 32-1303(1)).
- Resignation (no contract exceeding 30 days): the earlier of the next regular
  payday or 7 days (§ 32-1303(2)).
- Labor-dispute suspension: next regular payday (§ 32-1303(3)).
- Liquidated damages: 10% of unpaid wages per working day of delay, capped at
  treble the unpaid wages (§ 32-1303).

**Permitted deductions**:

- Deductions require legal compulsion, court order, or the employee's written
  authorization; deductions for shortages, breakage, or loss are restricted
  (§ 32-1304 et seq.; verify current DOES guidance on deduction categories).

**Pay statement requirements**:

- Itemized statement of deductions each pay period; itemized listing of
  allowances claimed (§ 32-1008(b)). Payroll records must be maintained for
  3 years (§ 32-1008; verify whether the 3-year figure sits in § 32-1308 or
  the Mayor's rulemaking).

## 4. Pay Transparency and Equity

**Salary range disclosure**:

- Every job listing and position description must state the good-faith minimum
  and maximum projected salary or hourly pay, covering new positions,
  promotions, and transfer opportunities (§ 32-1453.01(a)(1)).
- Employers must disclose the existence of healthcare benefits to prospective
  employees before the first interview (§ 32-1453.01(a)(2)).
- Employers must post a workplace notice of rights under the Act
  (§ 32-1453.01(b)); enforcement sits with the Office of the Attorney General
  (§ 32-1454).

**Salary history ban**:

- Employers may not screen prospective employees based on wage history,
  require wage history to satisfy minimum or maximum criteria, or require
  disclosure of wage history as a condition of interview or continued
  candidacy (§ 32-1452(a)(4)).

**Equal pay**:

- The Wage Transparency Act prohibits paying wages that discriminate on the
  basis of protected traits (§ 32-1452); the D.C. Human Rights Act
  independently prohibits compensation discrimination across its protected
  classes (§ 2-1402.11).
- No pay-data reporting mandate on private employers identified (verify).

## 5. Leave and Time: Paid Sick Leave, Family Leave, FMLA, Interaction with Pay/Role Changes

**Accrued Sick and Safe Leave Act (ASSLA)**:

- Accrual rates: 1 hour per 37 hours worked, capped at 7 days per calendar
  year (employers with 100+ employees); 1 per 43 hours, 5 days (25–99
  employees and tipped restaurant/bar employees regardless of size); 1 per 87
  hours, 3 days (24 or fewer employees) (§ 32-531.02(a)).
- Employee headcount is the average monthly FTE count for the prior calendar
  year (§ 32-531.02(a)(4)).
- Leave is usable no earlier than 90 days into employment; employees accrue
  from hire; unused accrued leave carries over (verify cap mechanics in §
  32-531.02(b) and DOES rules).
- Interaction with promotion: accrued leave balances follow the employee;
  employer size is measured at the employer level, so a worker's promotion does
  not change the accrual tier (verify whether the tipped-employee rule follows
  the role rather than the person when a tipped worker is promoted to a
  non-tipped role).

**Universal Paid Leave (UPLA)**:

- Employer payroll contribution of 0.75% of covered wages funds a
  District-administered benefit; up to 12 weeks of benefits for qualifying
  parental, family-care, or medical events; no employer size floor —
  contributions apply to all covered employers (§ 32-541.01 et seq.; DOES).
- UPL is a wage-replacement benefit, not job-protected leave; job protection
  derives from DCFMLA (16 weeks family + 16 weeks medical per 24 months for
  20+-employee employers, § 32-501 et seq.).

**Other mandated leave**:

- Voting leave: 2 hours paid (verify current statute, § 1-1001.07a family).
- School-leave and jury-duty protections exist in limited forms (verify scope
  for private employers).

## 6. Records and Access: Retention, Employee Access, Format

**Retention**:

- Payroll records: name, address, occupation, hours worked each day/week,
  rate of pay, deductions, and net/gross wages retained at least 3 years
  (§ 32-1008; verify exact subsection and whether WTPAA raised the period).
- Signed Notice of Hire acknowledgments must be retained as proof of
  compliance (§ 32-1008(c)).

**Employee access to personnel files**:

- No private-sector statutory right to inspect or copy a personnel file;
  public employees have separate access channels (verify CMPA applicability).
- Records must be open to the Mayor/DOES inspection on request
  (§ 32-1008(a)).

## 7. Privacy and Data: Employee Data Privacy, Biometric/Monitoring, Breach Notification, Residency

- No general consumer-privacy statute on the California model identified; the
  D.C. data-privacy landscape is limited to the breach-notification statute and
  sector rules (verify as of research date).
- Biometric information collected for timekeeping has no dedicated D.C.
  statute; breach of biometric data falls under the breach law when it meets
  the definition of personal information (§ 28-3851).
- **Breach notification**: notify affected residents as expeditiously as
  possible and without unreasonable delay; notify the Attorney General in
  writing when the breach affects 50 or more D.C. residents; provide at least
  18 months of identity-theft protection services at no cost when the breach
  involves a Social Security number or taxpayer identification number
  (§ 28-3852, Security Breach Protection Amendment Act of 2019).
- No data-residency requirement identified (verify).

## 8. Hiring and Background: Ban-the-Box, Background Checks, Drug Testing, E-Verify

**Ban-the-box**:

- Employers with 11 or more employees may not ask about or require disclosure
  of an applicant's criminal record until after a conditional offer of
  employment (Fair Criminal Record Screening Amendment Act of 2014, D.C. Code
  § 32-1341 et seq.); exceptions for positions where a clean record is required
  by law.

**Credit checks**:

- Employers may not use or require a prospective or current employee's credit
  information except where required by law or for positions involving security
  clearance, government contracts, or fiduciary duties (Fair Credit in
  Employment Amendment Act, D.C. Code § 2-1361.01 et seq.; verify exact
  scope).

**Drug testing**:

- Employers may not test a prospective employee for marijuana as a condition
  of employment and may not take adverse action for lawful cannabis use,
  except for safety-sensitive positions (Cannabis Employment Protections
  Amendment Act of 2022, D.C. Law 24-109, applicable from 2023).
- No general private-sector drug-testing statute; testing regimes are a
  matter of employer policy within the cannabis limit (verify).

**E-Verify**:

- No District mandate for private employers (federal contractors subject to
  federal rules).

## 9. Separation: Mini-WARN, Severance, Notice, Non-Compete/Non-Solicit

**Mini-WARN**:

- No District mini-WARN; federal WARN applies (verify no pending 2025–2026
  bill).

**Severance and notice**:

- No statutory severance or individual notice obligation beyond the final-pay
  timing rules (§ 32-1303).

**Non-compete**:

- Non-competes entered on or after 2022-10-01 are void for employees below the
  minimum qualifying annual compensation: $150,000, or $250,000 for medical
  specialists, adjusted annually by CPI from 2024 (§ 32-581.02;
  § 32-581.01(13)).
- For highly compensated employees, a compliant agreement requires the
  statutory written notice ("The District of Columbia Ban on Non-Compete
  Agreements Amendment Act of 2020 limits the use of non-compete
  agreements...") within prescribed windows — 14 days before employment begins
  or before the agreement is executed for a current employee (§ 32-581.03).
- Medical specialists additionally require a 365-day cap on duration
  (§ 32-581.02(c); verify).
- Non-solicitation of the employer's other employees and clients, and
  confidentiality provisions, survive as workplace-policy carve-outs to the
  extent the 2022 Clarification Amendment preserves them (§ 32-581.01;
  verify current scope for "anti-moonlighting" policies).
- Broadcast employees are excluded from the highly-compensated definition
  (§ 32-581.01(10)).

## 10. Classification and Multi-State: Contractor Tests, Remote Workers, Reciprocity

- **Wage/hour misclassification**: the Workplace Fraud Act applies an economic
  dependency test in the construction, landscaping, and related industries
  (D.C. Code § 32-1331 et seq.); other industries follow the common-law /
  economic-reality analysis under the Minimum Wage Act.
- **Multi-state**: a worker who spends 50%+ of working time in the District is
  covered by ASSLA; UPLA contribution follows D.C. wages; the District is not
  a party to an interstate tax reciprocity for nonresidents (verify remote-
  worker edge rules for MD/VA commuters, who pay tax to their residence
  state).
- **Jurisdiction note**: the District is a single coextensive jurisdiction —
  no counties, cities, or other local subdivisions; every locality-style
  overlay for D.C. resolves to the District itself, never to a locality of
  Maryland or Virginia.

## 11. Implications for P1A/P1B (HCM Platform Modeling)

**Required typed obligations and timing constraints**:

1. **Wage floor**: enforce $17.95/hr (2025-07-01 to 2026-06-30) and $18.40/hr
   (2026-07-01 forward); tipped roles carry the $10.00/$10.30 base with the
   weekly top-up rule; annual July-1 re-index.
2. **Hire/change notice (WTPAA)**: emit the Notice of Hire at hire and a
   revised written notice within 30 days when a promotion or base-pay change
   alters the stated rate, payday, or employer fields; retain signed
   acknowledgments; flag the $500-per-employee exposure.
3. **Pay transparency**: block or flag any posting, promotion notice, or
   transfer listing lacking a good-faith min/max pay range; ban wage-history
   screening fields; require healthcare-benefits disclosure before the first
   interview for external pipelines.
4. **Leave interaction**: carry accrued sick/safe leave across the pay or
   role change; employer-size tier derives from employer FTE count, not the
   worker; UPLA contribution tracks covered wages (a raise raises the
   contribution base).
5. **Non-compete recheck**: a promotion may lift an employee above the
   minimum qualifying annual compensation — recheck agreement validity; a
   fresh non-compete presented at promotion must satisfy the compensation
   threshold and notice windows.
6. **Final pay**: on discharge, the next-working-day deadline; on
   resignation, earlier of next payday or 7 days; compute the liquidated-
   damages exposure (10%/working day, treble cap).
7. **Pay frequency**: at least twice monthly; a promotion that moves a worker
   between frequency-eligible classes must not break the twice-monthly floor.
8. **Retention**: payroll records and signed notices for 3 years.
9. **Ban-the-box / cannabis**: internal-move pipelines that run background
   screens must respect the conditional-offer sequence; no marijuana
   pre-employment testing except safety-sensitive roles.
10. **Breach**: AG notification trigger at 50+ D.C. residents and the
    18-month SSN identity-theft-protection duty apply to employee data as well
    as customer data.

**Field restrictions and validations**:

- Wage-history fields: locked for D.C.-cited candidates (§ 32-1452).
- Salary-range fields on postings/transfers: required when the work location
  resolves to the District.
- Notice timing: the 30-day post-change update clock starts at the change's
  effective date.

**Open questions for legal review**:

- Confirm the WTPAA record-retention period and subsection (3 years).
- Verify whether any D.C. Council session act after 2024-06-30 amended the
  wage-transparency exemptions.
- Verify the 2026 indexed minimum-qualifying-compensation figure for the
  non-compete carve-out (CPI-adjusted from $150,000).
- Confirm whether the Workplace Fraud Act's misclassification test has been
  extended beyond construction-adjacent industries.
- Verify D.C. paid-leave benefit caps as of 2026 (weekly cap adjusted
  annually).
